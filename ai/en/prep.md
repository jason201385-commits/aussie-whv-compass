# Prepare, arrive and set up your first week

[Section permalink](https://www.aussiewhvcompass.com/lang/en/prep/#main-content)

A passport-neutral sequence for the decisions that are expensive or difficult to reverse. Sources checked 2026-08

**Editorial status:** this is a complete English editorial draft, not an Australian Government publication and not yet reviewed by a native-speaking Australian settlement or consumer-services professional. Official agencies, your visa grant and each service provider's current terms always control.

**Keep control of the process.** No paid intermediary is required to lodge a TFN application, open an ordinary bank account or complete an Incoming Passenger Card. Use the official links on this page and never send a stranger your passport scan, login code or banking password.

## 1. Before the visa grant: prepare, but do not lock yourself in

[Section permalink](https://www.aussiewhvcompass.com/lang/en/prep/#before-grant)

Home Affairs says not to book travel until you have written confirmation that the visa has been granted. Processing times are estimates, applications are assessed individually and requests for more information arrive through ImmiAccount.

- Keep saving and research more than one arrival city, but avoid a non-refundable flight, long lease or job resignation that depends on a guessed grant date.

- Check the passport used in the application. If it changes, update Home Affairs before travel.

- Read messages in your own ImmiAccount. Do not rely on a social-media agent saying the visa is approved.

Sources: [Home Affairs — WHM latest news](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/what-we-do/whm-program/latest-news) · [Check twice, submit once](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/check-twice-submit-once) | checked 2026-08-29

## 2. After grant: make a small, recoverable document pack

[Section permalink](https://www.aussiewhvcompass.com/lang/en/prep/#documents)

**Do not accidentally replace your WHM visa.** You do not need a separate visa to enter Australia after a 417 or 462 grant. If Home Affairs later grants an eVisitor (subclass 651) or Transit visa (subclass 771), your WHM visa and its work rights will cease. An ETA (subclass 601) is treated differently and does not cease the WHM visa, but it is still not needed for WHM entry. Safest: do not apply for any other Australian visa while holding a WHM unless you first check how the grant would affect the visa you intend to use. Check your current visa and conditions in VEVO before travel, especially if you applied for any other Australian visa.

- **Read the whole grant notice.** Check the passport details, first-entry deadline, visa period and conditions. Save an offline copy and one secure backup.

- **Travel on the linked passport.** Home Affairs warns that an unreported passport change can delay boarding. Carry a copy of the grant notice; it is useful even though the visa is electronic.

- **Prepare evidence without oversharing it.** Keep insurance, qualifications and employment references in a private folder. Send only the minimum document needed to a verified employer or service.

- **Preview the Incoming Passenger Card.** It must normally be completed in English. ABF publishes translated samples to help you understand the questions, but the submitted card itself is completed in English.

## 3. Health cover is passport-specific; insurance is activity-specific

[Section permalink](https://www.aussiewhvcompass.com/lang/en/prep/#insurance)

Australia has Reciprocal Health Care Agreements with 11 countries. Eligibility depends on where you lived before arriving and the agreement's conditions; the cover is limited to medically necessary care and is not the same as comprehensive travel or working-holiday insurance.

- Check the official RHCA list and the page for your country. Do not assume passport nationality alone makes you eligible.

- If eligible, follow your country's official page because access and proof differ. Ordinary visitors covered through New Zealand or Ireland do not enrol in Medicare or receive a Medicare card; they show the documents listed on their country page when seeking covered care.

- Whether or not RHCA applies, read the insurance Product Disclosure Statement. Confirm the policy period and cover for the work, driving, sports, manual labour, pre-existing conditions, medical evacuation and excess that apply to your plan.

- Save the insurer's emergency-assistance number and policy details offline. Know what requires pre-authorisation before treatment.

Sources: [Services Australia — Reciprocal Health Care Agreements](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/reciprocal-health-care-agreements) · [Services Australia — visiting from New Zealand](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/reciprocal-health-care-agreements-visiting-from-new-zealand?context=22481) · [Services Australia — visiting from Ireland](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/reciprocal-health-care-agreements-visiting-from-ireland?context=22481) · [Smartraveller — choosing travel insurance](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/travel-essentials/travel-insurance) | checked 2026-08-30

## 4. Build payment resilience, not a pile of cash

[Section permalink](https://www.aussiewhvcompass.com/lang/en/prep/#money)

- **Keep a runway.** Budget for temporary accommodation, food, local transport, a bond and time without work. The subclass 417 and 462 eligibility pages' minimum-funds test is not a promise that the same amount will cover your real arrival costs.

- **Use at least two payment paths.** Carry a modest amount of cash plus cards or accounts that you have already tested. Check foreign transaction, ATM, transfer and replacement-card fees with each provider.

- **Report large monetary instruments.** If the total you carry into or out of Australia is AUD10,000 or more, including the foreign-currency equivalent and relevant bearer instruments, submit the AUSTRAC cross-border report. Reporting does not itself prohibit carrying the money.

- **Do not use informal exchange strangers.** A screenshot is not cleared money. Use regulated providers and confirm funds in your own account.

Sources: [Home Affairs — first 417 financial requirement](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/work-holiday-417/first-working-holiday-417) · [Home Affairs — first 462 financial requirement](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/work-holiday-462/first-work-holiday-462) · [AUSTRAC — Cross-Border Movement of Monetary Instruments](https://online.apps.austrac.gov.au/cbmmi/carrying) | checked 2026-08-29

## 5. Medicines, food and outdoor gear: check first, declare when unsure

[Section permalink](https://www.aussiewhvcompass.com/lang/en/prep/#border)

**Declaring an item does not automatically mean losing it.** A biosecurity officer assesses declared goods. Failing to declare risk goods can lead to penalties and may affect immigration clearance.

- Check food, plant material, animal products, wooden articles and rural or freshwater equipment before packing. Clean and dry footwear and gear so it has no soil, seeds, grass or animal material.

- Declare risk goods on arrival or dispose of them in the terminal bins. If unsure, declare them.

- Under the TGA traveller's exemption, many personal medicines can be brought in their original labelled packaging with the prescription or a doctor's letter, generally up to a three-month supply. Some medicines and devices have extra controls, so check the exact product.

- Do not bring medicine for friends or sell your supply. Herbal and traditional medicines also need to satisfy Australian import and biosecurity rules.

## 6. Driving rules change by state or territory

[Section permalink](https://www.aussiewhvcompass.com/lang/en/prep/#driving)

Many temporary visitors can drive for a period using a current overseas licence, but the time limit, translation requirements and conversion rules are not nationally identical. New South Wales, Victoria and the Northern Territory have specific time limits that differ from several other jurisdictions.

- Before driving, open the licensing authority for the state or territory where you will live and confirm the current visitor rule.

- Carry the original overseas licence. If it is not in English, check whether that jurisdiction requires an approved translation, an International Driving Permit, or either.

- An IDP is a translation companion, not a replacement licence. Rental-company acceptance does not prove you meet the road-law requirement.

- Check vehicle registration, insurance, roadworthiness and your own insurance before buying or driving a vehicle.

Source: [Austroads — Overseas Drivers and state or territory authority links](https://austroads.gov.au/drivers-and-vehicles/overseas-drivers) | checked 2026-08-29

## 7. First 72 hours: create time to make safe decisions

[Section permalink](https://www.aussiewhvcompass.com/lang/en/prep/#first-72)

- **Activate a phone service.** Keep your new number, accommodation address and emergency contacts offline as well as in the phone. Call 000 only for an emergency; do not make a test call.

- **Use official local transport information.** Each city has its own card, fares and airport route. Check the relevant transport authority rather than an old travel post.

- **Keep temporary accommodation for a buffer.** A practical safer default is 7–14 nights with clear cancellation terms, giving you time to inspect longer-term housing and verify who can rent it to you.

- **Do not pay an unseen rental.** Scamwatch warns that fake landlords use real addresses, refuse inspection and demand advance bond or rent. Inspect, verify the agent or owner independently and use the state or territory bond process.

- **Start the bank-account process through the bank's own site or branch.** Requirements vary. Verify the domain and never hand an intermediary your banking password or one-time code.

Source: [Scamwatch — rental scams](https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/types-of-scams/buying-and-selling-scams) | checked 2026-08-29

## 8. First week: TFN, bank, myGov and super in a safe order

[Section permalink](https://www.aussiewhvcompass.com/lang/en/prep/#first-week)

- **Apply for a TFN directly with the ATO.** Eligible foreign passport holders with work rights can apply online after entering Australia. The application is free. Use only the ATO route and save the reference.

- **You can start work while the TFN is pending.** Tell the employer on the TFN declaration that you have applied. ATO rules give 28 days to provide it; if you neither quote a TFN nor claim an exemption nor say you applied, a foreign-resident employee can be withheld at 45%.

- **Open and secure the bank account.** Give payroll only the BSB and account number needed for wages, never your login or verification code. Add the TFN to the bank after it arrives if the bank asks for it for interest withholding.

- **Optional first-week setup: create myGov and link the ATO when useful.** This is not required before you can start work. Type the address yourself and do not use an unsolicited employment message to sign in.

- **Understand super before onboarding.** Eligible employees can usually choose a fund. If you do not choose, the employer may need to use an existing stapled fund before its default fund. Multiple accounts can mean multiple fees; compare through official tools before consolidating because insurance may be lost.

## 10. Five mistakes that make arrival harder

[Section permalink](https://www.aussiewhvcompass.com/lang/en/prep/#avoid)

| Mistake | Safer default |
| --- | --- |
| Booking non-refundable travel before the visa grant | Wait for the written grant and read every date and condition. |
| Assuming Medicare or the cheapest policy covers everything | Check RHCA eligibility and read the insurance PDS for your actual work and activities. |
| Hiding food, medicine or dirty outdoor gear | Check the item, clean equipment and declare when unsure. |
| Paying bond or rent because accommodation feels urgent | Use a short buffer stay, inspect and verify before paying. |
| Paying someone to obtain a TFN or using their login link | Apply free through the ATO and keep all account credentials private. |
