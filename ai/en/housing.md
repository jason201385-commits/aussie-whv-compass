# Accommodation and renting in Australia

[Section permalink](https://www.aussiewhvcompass.com/lang/en/housing/#main-content)

Secure a recoverable first stay, then inspect and verify before committing to a longer agreement. Sources checked 2026-08

**Editorial status:** this is a complete English editorial draft, not an Australian Government publication and not yet reviewed by a native-speaking Australian tenancy, housing or homelessness-services professional. Rental law, bond processes and the legal status of a room differ by state or territory and by agreement type. The linked authority for the place where you live always controls.

## If you need somewhere to stay now

[Section permalink](https://www.aussiewhvcompass.com/lang/en/housing/#quick-path)

- **Book a short, recoverable stay first.** Compare the final price, location, check-in time, cancellation terms and recent reviews on at least two services. Keep the booking and payment inside the verified platform.

- **Do not wire a long-term deposit from overseas.** A real address, copied licence, video call or familiar listing photo does not prove that the advertiser can rent the property.

- **Inspect before paying for a room or lease.** If that is impossible, use temporary accommodation until you or a trusted person can inspect inside and verify the person offering it.

- **Open the official guide for your state or territory.** Residential tenancy, rooming or boarding accommodation, a hostel, a sublet and employer-provided housing may follow different rules.

- **No safe place or no money for a booking?** [Ask Izzy](https://askizzy.org.au/) can help you search nearby housing and crisis-support services. It is a service directory, not a booking guarantee.

- **Immediate danger:** call 000 and move to a safe place. Do not confront a suspected scammer, violent housemate or exploiter alone.

## 2. Name the arrangement before you compare the price

[Section permalink](https://www.aussiewhvcompass.com/lang/en/housing/#choose)

| Arrangement | Useful for | Confirm before paying |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hostel, hotel or serviced stay | First nights, uncertain arrival time or city testing | Total price, room type, check-in access, luggage, cancellation and platform support |
| Room in a share house | Lower setup cost and flexible location | Who is on the lease, permission to sublet, house rules, bills, notice, bond handling and who can enter |
| Whole-property tenancy | More control and a formal household | Named tenants, term, rent, bond route, utilities, condition report, strata rules and break-lease consequences |
| Rooming, boarding or student-style accommodation | Individual room with shared facilities or services | Which local legal category applies, house rules, provider duties, fees and complaint route |
| Employer or working-hostel accommodation | Remote work or transport-linked roles | Job and housing terms separately, deductions, transport, what happens when shifts stop and how you can leave |

For a longer stay, compare current weekly asking prices for similar rooms or properties in the same suburb on at least two listing services. A price far below comparable current listings is a scam warning, not proof of a bargain.

**The label in an advertisement is not the legal test.** “Share house”, “board”, “licence”, “guest” or “staff accommodation” can have different consequences depending on the facts and local law. Describe the arrangement to the relevant authority if you are unsure which rules apply.

## 3. Inspect the property, the commute and the people

[Section permalink](https://www.aussiewhvcompass.com/lang/en/housing/#inspect)

- **Verify access.** Meet at the property, inspect inside and check that keys, room and shared areas match the advertisement. A drive-by or exterior video is not enough.

- **Verify the advertiser.** For an agency listing, find the agency website independently and call its published number. For a private or sublet room, ask who owns or rents the property and what authority permits your occupation.

- **Check the practical total.** Ask what rent includes, how utilities are divided, minimum stay, notice, bond, internet, laundry, parking, heating or cooling and replacement-key fees.

- **Test the journey.** Check the actual first and last public-transport options for likely shifts. A cheap remote room can become expensive if it requires a car or unsafe late-night travel.

- **Inspect safety and condition.** Look at door and window locks, smoke alarms, exits, mould, pests, water, power, cooking, bathroom access, overcrowding and anything promised for repair. Record concerns before agreeing.

- **Meet the household.** Confirm who lives there, visitors, smoking, pets, cleaning, noise, shared food, couples, parking and how decisions or conflicts are handled.

**Leave first if fire escape looks unsafe.** Do not stay where exits are blocked or sealed, smoke alarms appear to be missing, or crowding makes escape unsafe. If there is immediate danger, call 000. Otherwise ask the local council or state fire or building-safety authority how to report it; reporting routes and legal standards differ by location.

Fire-safety example, not a national rule: [Queensland Fire Department — budget accommodation buildings](https://www.fire.qld.gov.au/compliance-and-planning/budget-accommodation-buildings) | checked 2026-08-30

**Protect identity documents.** Ask why each document is needed, who will receive it and how long it will be kept. Provide the minimum needed only after verifying the property and recipient. Do not send a banking password, email password, myGov login or one-time code to obtain housing.

## 4. Read the agreement, rent and bond route before money moves

[Section permalink](https://www.aussiewhvcompass.com/lang/en/housing/#agreement)

- Get the proposed agreement and all special terms before signing. Match the property, parties, term, rent, payment account, bond, included items, bills, notice and promised repairs.

- Do not sign a blank or incomplete agreement. Keep the signed copy, rules, receipts and every later change in one private folder.

- Confirm the official bond process for your state or territory. In some places a renter can pay the authority directly; in others the landlord or agent receives and lodges it. Do not invent a national rule.

- Use a traceable payment route to the verified party. Check that the account name and instructions match the agreement; independently confirm any last-minute account change.

- Do not assume an informal share room has no rules or rights. Head tenants, subtenants, boarders and lodgers may have different obligations, and an unauthorised sublet can put your housing at risk.

- Before a long fixed term, check the cost and notice process if work, visa timing, health or location changes. Do not rely on “you can leave whenever” unless the written agreement and local rules support it.

Cross-state reference points: [NSW — signing and moving in](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/renting-a-place-to-live/starting-a-residential-tenancy/signing-a-lease-and-moving) · [Victoria — signing an agreement](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/starting-and-changing-rental-agreements/applying-signing-and-moving-in/knowing-your-rights-when-signing-an-agreement) · [Queensland — rental bond](https://www.rta.qld.gov.au/starting-a-tenancy/rental-bond) · [WA — rental bonds](https://www.consumerprotection.wa.gov.au/rental-bonds) | checked 2026-08-30

## 5. Make move-in evidence before daily life erases it

[Section permalink](https://www.aussiewhvcompass.com/lang/en/housing/#condition)

- Read the official deadline and method for your location. Condition-report deadlines are not the same across Australia.

- Compare every room, fitting, appliance, key and included item with the supplied report. Add anything dirty, damaged, missing, unsafe or not working.

- Take clear, dated photos or video of walls, floors, furniture, appliances, mould, meter readings and every existing defect. Keep the original files.

- Return the report through a traceable channel within the official deadline and keep your copy plus proof of delivery.

- Put promised repairs in writing. A spoken promise does not change the evidence of what existed when you moved in.

**This protects both sides.** NSW, Victoria, Queensland, WA, SA, Tasmania, the ACT and NT all publish official condition-report or start-of-tenancy guidance, but their forms and deadlines differ.

Examples: [NSW condition reports](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/rules/rental-property-condition-reports) · [Victoria condition reports](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/rent-bond-bills-and-condition-reports/condition-reports) · [WA property condition reports](https://www.consumerprotection.wa.gov.au/property-condition-reports) · [Tasmania condition reports](https://cbos.tas.gov.au/topics/housing/renting/beginning-tenancy/condition-reports) | checked 2026-08-30

## 7. Separate your job decision from your housing decision

[Section permalink](https://www.aussiewhvcompass.com/lang/en/housing/#work-housing)

- Ask for the job offer, pay, hours, transport and accommodation price as separate written terms. A promise of future shifts is not payment for present rent.

- Ask what happens to the room if shifts are cancelled, you resign, the employer ends the job or transport fails. Keep enough control and money to leave.

- Do not surrender your passport. An employer can verify work rights, but it does not need to keep your original passport.

- Check every accommodation, transport or meal deduction on the payslip. Fair Work says pay deductions are permitted only in limited situations; employee authorisation must be genuine and some deductions that benefit the employer must also be reasonable.

- Never buy specified-work records, payslips or a promise of a second or third WHM visa. Housing does not turn ineligible work into eligible specified work.

## 8. Repairs, disputes, unsafe housing and leaving

[Section permalink](https://www.aussiewhvcompass.com/lang/en/housing/#problems)

- **Preserve the record.** Save the agreement, bond acknowledgement, rent receipts, condition report, photos, repair requests, notices and a dated timeline.

- **Use the correct notice.** Do not copy a notice period from another state, a different agreement type or an old forum post. Open your authority's current form and process.

- **Urgent repairs or unsafe conditions:** follow the local urgent-repair route. If there is immediate danger, call 000 and leave the danger first.

- **Bond disagreement:** do not sign a blank bond-release form or accept an unsupported deduction just to end the conversation. Use the authority's bond claim or dispute route and submit your evidence on time.

- **Violence, threats, coercion or restricted movement:** prioritise a safe exit. Do not confront the person alone. For immediate danger call 000; for confidential national support use [1800RESPECT](https://1800respect.org.au/). Rental authorities publish special tenancy routes in some family-violence situations.

- **Need an interpreter to contact a service?** Check the [TIS National contact options](https://www.tisnational.gov.au/en/Contact-us), then confirm access and any charge with the service you need to call.

- **Possible scam:** contact the payment provider quickly, secure accounts and use the [English recovery sequence](https://www.aussiewhvcompass.com/lang/en/scam/#already-happened).
